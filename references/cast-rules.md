# Cast rules

How the registry works, how to pick a cast, and how registers keep a mixed-proportion picture readable.

## The registry

`assets/characters/character-registry.json` (schema version 2) is the authority. Every character has:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `id` | `cNN` (or `whalechan`); the only stable handle |
| `display_name` | the name used in prose and prompts |
| `aliases` | Chinese and descriptive aliases; all resolve to the same id |
| `kind` | flavour label (`human`, `dragon`, `divine-beast`, `vampire`, …) |
| `asset` | `{path, sha256, media_type, bytes, width, height}`; verified on load |
| `primary_reference` | must equal `asset.path` |
| `identity_lock` | the must-preserve identity sentence |
| `signature_elements` | the elements that make her recognisable |
| `palette` | her own hue family |
| `prompt_block` | the verbatim `REGISTERED CHARACTER …` identity block copied into prompts |
| `negative` | the must-not list copied into prompts |
| `drawn_ratio` | head ratio of her own sheet, as an estimate |
| `register` | `chibi` / `compact` / `mature` |
| `proportion_policy` | `as-drawn` / `redraw` / `mature` |
| `draw_note` | how to translate her sheet into another register |
| `personality` | how to play her in a picture; copied into the prompt verbatim as a `personality:` line. A neutral placeholder means "no extra direction" |

The two skills `whalechan-image-character` and `whalechan-image-comic` keep their own five-form catalogues untouched. This library is a separate, additive layer; it never enters those catalogues and never changes their counts.

## Choosing a cast

- Use the smallest cast that carries the beat. Two characters in a clean composition beat five in a crowd.
- Every in-frame character needs a reason in the concept table; if the reason is "she is popular", cut her.
- Characters can be `offscreen` or `absent` to keep the narrative complete without drawing them.
- Maximum 6 per picture. Split the picture rather than shrinking everyone.

## Registers and scale

A picture freezes one head ratio (or one per character in `mixed`). Then, per character:

- Already in the target register → only overall scale changes; the design stays.
- Mature character forced into a smaller register → re-proportion, never age down. Keep the adult face construction; shorten limbs and simplify drapery instead of shrinking the whole figure.
- `chibi` character in a `mature` picture → the least desirable combination. Prefer a `compact` shared register, or make the chibi character smaller and put her at the edge rather than restyling her.

The register instruction is generated by `manage-run.py` and copied verbatim into the prompt. Do not paraphrase it.

## Style consistency across a cast

The picture carries one art preset and one sentence: every character shares line weight, shading step count, highlight treatment and contrast level. Individual hue families stay, individual rendering styles do not. Never let a character keep her own shading or line treatment, and never let the preset override the identity lock.

## Personality

Personality lives in the registry, not in the concept table, so it cannot drift between runs. `build-prompt.py` copies each in-frame character's `personality` into the prompt verbatim, right under her register lines, so a trait like Kimi's tsundere sore-loser play reaches the model on every page.

- A trait describes **how to play her**, never what she looks like. Appearance stays in `prompt_block` and `identity_lock`.
- Write it as performance direction: what the face and body do, and what they must not do. `c04 Kimi` is registered as a tsundere sore loser: proud little smile, flustered pout, cheeks puffed, looking away, arms crossed, a smile she is trying to swallow — never mean, never genuinely angry, never a hostile glare.
- Keep it short. Two or three sentences; longer text dilutes the identity lock.
- Change a character's personality by editing the registry, not by rewriting prompts. The registry hash is frozen at `init`, so a change after freezing is detected rather than silently applied.

## What a reference sheet may and may not control

A registered sheet controls identity only. It never controls background, pose, framing, head ratio, text or composition. Its sample sparkles, props and motifs are not automatically part of the picture — the identity lock says which ones are.
